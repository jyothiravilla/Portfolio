My Path

In 10th grade, Darsha Academy visited my school and gave an aptitude test. Darsha is a girls’ education and empowerment program started by Eventide Asset Management in Boston. I passed, and was offered a place in their fully funded residential program, where I was prepared for India’s CET and JEE exams.

My goals pointed somewhere else: I wanted to study in the United States. When I told the team, they didn’t steer me back. They trained me for the SAT. That led to Arcadia University, where I earned my BSc in Computer Science with a minor in Math, and then to WashU’s dual-degree program in St. Louis, where I’m now a senior. Bloom India has supported my WashU tuition, and Darsha has stood behind me throughout.

In summer 2023, I interned as a software developer at Eventide itself, which was a special way to work alongside the people who made this path possible. I don’t take any of it lightly, and it’s why I try to do every project, from cloud systems to surgical-device firmware, as well as I can.

Firmware Engineering Intern

Jun – Aug 2026

Bausch + Lomb · St. Louis, MO

Designed closed-loop stepper motor control firmware for a surgical device, using a TMC2160 motor driver to precisely position a mechanical component, one with no physical limit switch to detect when it reached the end of its travel.

Built a stall-detection homing routine that used the driver's built-in load-feedback to infer that limit electronically instead. Without it, the motor would keep driving past the mechanical stop, binding the gear train under continued torque. Most of the effort went into tuning the stall threshold so it triggered reliably at the true limit without false-triggering during normal operation.

Separately, contributed to the software requirements, architecture, and design documentation (SRS/SAS/SDD) for a glaucoma surgery platform, the paperwork that defines what the software is supposed to do and how it's built, which regulated medical devices require by FDA before they can ship.
